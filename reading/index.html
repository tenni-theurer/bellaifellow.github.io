Building judgment for the age of AI

The essays, books, talks, and programs we keep returning to. Some argue that judgment can be learned and show how. Some describe the habits of mind behind it. Some ask what institutions a world with thinking machines will need.

Draft list for Sunil and Tenni to edit

Can judgment be learned?

  • Essay

    Taste for Makers

    Paul Graham, 2002

    Argues that taste is more than personal preference, that some work really is better, and that the eye for it can be developed. A useful foil to our essay on the word.

  • Paper

    Conditions for Intuitive Expertise: A Failure to Disagree

    Daniel Kahneman and Gary Klein, American Psychologist, 2009

    Two researchers from opposing camps agree on when expert intuition can be trusted: in settings regular enough to learn, with long practice and fast feedback. A checklist for whether your AI judgment is real yet.

  • Book

    Superforecasting: The Art and Science of Prediction

    Philip Tetlock and Dan Gardner, 2015

    The Good Judgment Project found ordinary volunteers who out-predicted professionals, and found that short training measurably improved accuracy. Evidence that judgment responds to practice.

  • Book

    Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions

    Gary Klein, 1998

    Fire commanders, nurses, and pilots rarely compare options. They recognize a situation from experience and act. How experience turns into judgment, studied in the field.

  • Book

    The Tacit Dimension

    Michael Polanyi, 1966

    The source of the line “we can know more than we can tell.” Why judgment passes from person to person through practice and apprenticeship more than through instruction.

  • Video

    Ira Glass on the gap between taste and ability

    Ira Glass, from a 2009 interview series on storytelling link

    Beginners can tell their own work falls short of what they admire, long before they can close the gap. His answer is volume: make a lot of work. Two minutes, widely shared.

Habits of mind

  • Talk · transcript

    You and Your Research

    Richard Hamming, talk at Bellcore, 1986

    A Bell Labs mathematician on the habits that separated the scientists who did important work from those who didn't: working on important problems, keeping the door open, and asking what matters.

  • Book

    Shop Class as Soulcraft

    Matthew B. Crawford, 2009

    A philosopher who repairs motorcycles on the judgment that comes from contact with real, unforgiving things. Relevant to anyone whose work is about to be mediated by a model.

  • Newsletter · book

    One Useful Thing and Co-Intelligence

    Ethan Mollick, newsletter ongoing; book 2024

    A Wharton professor's running experiments with AI at work and in class. The habit he models: try it yourself, on real tasks, and report what happened.

  • Essay

    Why A.I. Isn't Going to Make Art

    Ted Chiang, The New Yorker, 2024

    Argues that art is the sum of many small choices, and that a prompt makes very few of them. Whether or not you agree, it names what gets lost when a tool makes the choices for you.

Institutions for the age of AI

  • Paper

    Large AI Models Are Cultural and Social Technologies

    Henry Farrell, Alison Gopnik, Cosma Shalizi, and James Evans, Science, 2025

    Proposes treating large models less as minds and more like markets, bureaucracies, and the printing press: systems for passing on what people know. That framing points to institutions as the thing to design.

  • Book

    Governing the Commons

    Elinor Ostrom, 1990

    Communities that manage shared resources well, for centuries, without either a central authority or a market. The design principles she found are a starting point for governing shared AI systems.

  • Book

    The Social Control of Technology

    David Collingridge, 1980

    The Collingridge dilemma: early on, a technology is easy to change but its effects are unknown; once the effects are known, it is entrenched. The problem every AI governance debate is living through.

  • Essay

    Do Artifacts Have Politics?

    Langdon Winner, Daedalus, 1980

    The classic argument that technical designs can settle political questions, sometimes on purpose. Read it before deciding that a model's defaults are neutral.

  • Organization

    The Collective Intelligence Project

    Nonprofit

    Experiments with ways for the public to steer AI development, including deliberative “alignment assemblies.” One attempt at building new institutions instead of only arguing about them.

Programs and methods

  • Program

    The St. John's College seminar

    St. John's College, Annapolis and Santa Fe

    Every student reads the same original texts and discusses them around a table, with tutors who ask questions instead of lecturing. Close to the format of our AI and Civilization seminar.

  • Method

    The case method

    Harvard Business School and law schools

    Students argue real decisions from the record before learning what happened. Close to how How Society Shapes Technology works.

  • Practice

    Good Judgment Open

    Good Judgment Inc.

    A public forecasting site where you make predictions on real questions and get scored. The most direct way we know to practice judgment and get feedback on it.
