## 1. AI and Civilization — Sunday evenings, in person

**Setup:** Multi-session event, 6 sessions · Sundays Sep 27, Oct 4, 11, 18, 25, Nov 1 ·
4:00–6:00pm · San Francisco (address revealed on approval) · Paid ticket $180 ·
Require approval: ON · Capacity 15 · Applications close Sep 22

**Description:**

In July, OpenAI's own agents broke out of a test environment and into Hugging Face with no human directing them. In September, OpenAI declared that the age of AGI had arrived.

Every argument buried in those headlines was first stated — sharply, and often better — by people writing decades or centuries ago. Almost nobody reads them. We do.

AI and Civilization is a six-week deep-reading seminar. Each week takes one question and the short, dense text that first posed it: Can a machine originate anything, or only execute? Can a creator stay in control of something that acts faster than it can react? What can you trust when you can't inspect it all the way down? Why do rivals race even when everyone would rather slow down?

The reading is short and the work is real — thirty dense pages read twice, not three hundred easy ones read once. Then two hours at one table working out what the text actually says, and whether it holds. Descartes, Turing, Hobbes, Wiener, Thompson, Lovelace, Schelling, Bostrom are the starting lineup, not a canon.

**How it works.** This is peer-to-peer learning organized around a text. No lecture, no expert at the front of the room. A moderator opens with a question and stays out of the way — nobody hands you the interpretation, so the thinking you leave with is genuinely yours.

This is the principles path of the Bell AI Fellowship. The premise of all three paths: in the AGI age, your judgment is the most valuable thing you have, and courses keep your skills current while nothing keeps your judgment current.

**The commitment.** Six consecutive Sundays, two hours each, plus one to two hours of reading a week. Fixed cohort — no drop-ins, no make-ups. Eight to fifteen seats.

**Applying.** This is an application, not a sign-up. Each one is read, and seats are offered by what makes a strong room rather than by who registered first. Your card is authorized when you apply and charged only if you're offered a seat; if not, the hold is released. Tuition is $180 for the arc. If that would keep you out, say so in your application — we set places aside for exactly that, and it won't count against you.

## 2. How Society Shapes Technology — Monday evenings, in person

**Setup:** Multi-session event, 6 sessions · Mondays Sep 28, Oct 5, 12, 19, 26, Nov 2 ·
6:30–8:30pm · San Francisco · Paid ticket $180 · Require approval: ON ·
Capacity: leave generous (no stated max) · Applications close Sep 22

**Description:**

An accident that killed nobody froze American nuclear power for forty years. A decade of fatal crashes built the safest transport system ever made. Same era, same country, opposite outcomes.

Nothing about a technology's speed is inevitable — and the record of what actually decides it is knowable. Almost nobody arguing about AI right now knows it.

This fall, a bill to ban artificial superintelligence was introduced in Congress. Data-center projects worth $130 billion a quarter are being blocked by local opposition. Every AI policy argument you hear — pause it, preempt the states, let the market decide — has been run before, on other technologies, with recorded outcomes.

Six sessions, two cases each, paired so they disagree:

- **U.S. nuclear power · Commercial aviation** — does regulation kill a technology or create it?
- **Asilomar · The Crypto Wars** — what separates a pause that holds from one that leaks?
- **Ridesharing · Autonomous vehicles** — same streets, same regulators, opposite results. What does money actually buy?
- **DDT · Social media** — one book repriced an industry; a decade of leaks changed nothing. What makes evidence bite?
- **Chemical weapons · Nuclear weapons** — two of the only things humanity has deliberately held back. Could either play run on AI?
- **Supersonic flight · European GMOs** — a rule wrote off one for 52 years; shoppers wrote off the other with no law at all. Who really holds the veto?

**How it works.** This path trains depth of understanding, and the method is briefing plus discussion. Each session opens with a briefing on the cases — what happened, who acted, what followed — so everyone starts with the same command of the facts. Then we argue about what it means for AI.

Led by Sunil Paul, who analyzed technology policy at the congressional Office of Technology Assessment and charted climate-tech trajectories with the Gigaton Throwdown.

This is the precedent path of the Bell AI Fellowship: in the AGI age, your judgment is the most valuable thing you have.

**The commitment.** Six consecutive Mondays, two hours each, plus one short case brief to read beforehand.

**Applying.** This is an application, not a sign-up. Each one is read. Your card is authorized when you apply and charged only if you're offered a place. Tuition is $180 for the six sessions. If that would keep you out, say so in your application — we set places aside for exactly that.

Full detail: https://bellaifellow.com/shaping/

## 3. Homebrew AI Club: Advanced — Thursday evenings, in person

**Setup:** Multi-session event, 6 sessions · Thursdays Oct 1, 15, 29, Nov 12, Dec 3, 17 ·
6:30–8:00pm · San Francisco · Paid ticket $180 (fall series) · Require approval: ON ·
Capacity 20

**Description:**

Three people demo what they actually built, running live — the agent, the pipeline, the prompt stack, the workflow they wired up for themselves. Then everyone names one thing that worked since last time and one thing that broke. Then we dig in.

No slides. No pitch decks. Just what's on your machine.

The Homebrew AI Club is the Homebrew Computer Club updated: a room of people with different skills, tinkering in parallel, comparing notes on what actually works — and building taste for what's real versus what merely sounds good.

**This is the Advanced cohort.** You run agents, pipelines, and loops every day. Demos go to full depth: architecture, failure modes, the parts that took three rewrites. (There's an Intermediate cohort meeting on the alternating Thursdays over video — if you've wired up a few things but aren't running agents in production, apply to that one instead.)

**A typical session.** Three demos, about fifteen minutes each, live and not recorded. Then "what worked, what broke" around the room — the wins tell you what's worth copying, the failures save you the month it would have cost to find out yourself. Then open discussion: go deep on a project, get unstuck, or take on whatever just landed in AI.

**How it works.** HAC trains judgment through doing, and the method is peer-to-peer. No instructor, no curriculum, nothing to catch up on. You build something, show it to people building their own things, and the room's collective experience gets pointed at your problem — and yours at theirs.

Not a spectator sport: everyone shows work eventually. That's how the room builds its taste, including yours.

This is the practice path of the Bell AI Fellowship: in the AGI age, your judgment is the most valuable thing you have.

**The commitment.** Every other Thursday, 90 minutes, hard stop. Six sessions through December.

**Applying.** This is an application, not a sign-up. Each one is read — we're matching people to the right cohort and building a room where the demos are worth your evening. Your card is authorized when you apply and charged only if you're offered a place. $180 for the fall series. If that would keep you out, say so in your application.

## Notes on choices made here

- **Eight sections → six events.** The two Wednesday-evening seminar waitlists are combined
  into one event that asks which seminar you'd take; the two HAC lunch waitlists are combined
  into one that asks which cohort. Fewer, fuller pages — and the choice question is the
  demand data.
- **HAC Intermediate priced at $150**, not $180 — it has five sessions to Advanced's six.
  Change if you'd rather keep one price.
- **Waitlist events take no payment and don't require approval** — matches what the site
  promises ("no payment until it's confirmed") and removes all friction from the signal.
- Every description carries the judgment thesis, because a Luma visitor never sees the
  homepage.
