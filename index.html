How Judgment Gets Built

Courses keep your skills current, but how do you build your judgment and keep it current? That's what this Fellowship is for. You grapple with real things, question them, talk them through with others, and learn from what works and what doesn't.

We have three paths. Pick any one of them — or all three.

Your own setups
Homebrew AI Club

A working session for people running real AI agents, tools, and loops. Members demo their setups live, everyone names what broke this month, and we dig into it together. Each cohort is calibrated to its level — one for people getting started, one for advanced practitioners.

See HAC →
The original texts
AI and Civilization

A deep-reading seminar on the texts today's AI arguments came from — among them Descartes, Turing, Hobbes, Wiener, Schelling. Short readings that repay a second pass; two hours working out what they actually say, together.

See the seminar →
The historical record
How Society Shapes Technology

Thirteen cases from 120 years of technology governance — what got sped up, redirected, or stopped, and why. Work through the record, argue the mapping: where will AI's trajectory actually be decided?

See the cases →

Each track is small and scheduled by who signs up — some sessions in person in San Francisco, some over video.

Everything else about AI, you can rent. The models, the tools, the agents — they're the same for you as for everyone else, and they get better whether you do or not. Your judgment is the part that's actually yours: what to ask, what to trust, what to hand off, what to keep for yourself. Call it discernment, wisdom, taste — it's the skill underneath every other skill now, and it appreciates with use.

How should humans train themselves in the face of a "thinking machine"?

That's the question this fellowship exists to answer. There's no course for judgment, because judgment isn't information. It's built in rooms like these.
