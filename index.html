Our Approach

Everything else about AI, you can rent. The models, the tools, the agents — they're the same for you as for everyone else, and they get better whether you do or not. Your judgment is the part that's actually yours: what to ask, what to trust, what to hand off, what to keep for yourself. Call it discernment, wisdom, taste — it's the skill underneath every other skill now, and it appreciates with use.

Each path works on that judgment from a different angle. In the Homebrew AI Club, it comes from practice: running agents and tools yourself, seeing what other people run, and learning as much from what breaks as from what works. In AI and Civilization, it comes from principles: today's arguments about machine minds, control, and trust were first staged by Descartes, Turing, Wiener, and their peers, and thinking through the originals sharpens how you weigh the echoes. In How Society Shapes Technology, it comes from precedent: 120 years of technologies sped up, redirected, or stopped shows what actually decides a technology's path — and what will decide AI's.

Practice, principles, precedent — three angles on the same skill: knowing what to use, what to trust, what to build, and where this is all going.

How should humans train themselves in the face of a "thinking machine"?

That's the question this fellowship exists to answer. There's no course for judgment, because judgment isn't information. It's built in rooms like these.
