Homebrew AI Club (HAC)

Build judgment through demos.

A working session for people to build their AI experience, expertise and judgment. We do it by doing the work and showing it to people who will celebrate your wins and tell you what's wrong with it.

what is HAC?

Members demo their real setups, live: the agent, the pipeline, the prompt stack, the experiment they ran. We discuss what worked and what broke since last time, and we dig in together.

Judgment comes from running things, breaking them, and working out the kinks, with people doing the same. Why does one setup hold and another fall apart? The focus is what is on your machine and discussion, not lectures or slideware.

How it works: HAC trains judgment through doing, and the method is peer-to-peer. There's no instructor and no curriculum. You build something, show it to people building their own things, and the room's collective experience gets pointed at your problem — and yours at theirs. What you leave with is a sharper sense of what works, learned from people who tried it. We curate the participants to be peers who can teach as well as learn.

It's the Homebrew Computer Club, updated: a room of people with different skills, tinkering together, comparing notes on what works, and building discernment and taste for what deserves to grow versus what just sounds good.

two cohorts

The same format, calibrated to the room. The cohorts alternate weeks, so yours meets every other week — twice a month — and you're always with peers. Participants come from all levels from CTOs and CEOs of technology companies to individual consultants and freelancers, to students and recent graduates.

int
Intermediate
You use AI daily. Maybe you've done more than prompting like made a custom GPT, a script, or an automation.  Demos of better prompts and tools are pitched so you can follow them and learn from them.
adv
Advanced
You run agents, pipelines, or loops every day. Maybe you're doing AI-based experiments and research. Demos go to full depth: architecture, failure modes, the parts that are still in development.

what it isn't

  • Not a course. No syllabus, no sequence, nothing to catch up on.
  • Not a lecture. Nobody stands at the front and explains AI to you.
  • Not a spectator sport. Everyone shows work eventually. That's how the room builds its taste — including yours.

a typical session

1
Three demos
~15 min each, live, the actual instance running (or breaking!)
2
What worked. What broke.
Everyone names one thing that paid off since last time and one that didn't. Both are useful: you find out what's worth copying, and what would have cost you weeks to learn the hard way.
3
Open discussion
Go deep on a specific project, get unstuck, or take on whatever just landed in AI — a new model, a new tool, a story worth arguing about
