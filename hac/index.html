Homebrew AI Club (HAC)

Build judgment through demos.

A working session for people running real AI agents, tools, and loops — where judgment gets built by doing the work, showing it, and digging into what breaks.

what is HAC?

Members demo their real setups, live: the agent, the pipeline, the prompt stack, the workflow they built for themselves. Then everyone names what worked and what broke this month. Then we dig in together.

You don't get judgment from a tutorial. You get it from running things, breaking them, and working out — with people doing the same — why one setup holds and another falls apart. No slides. No pitch decks. Just what's on your machine.

How it works: HAC trains judgment through doing, and the method is peer-to-peer. There's no instructor and no curriculum. You build something, show it to people building their own things, and the room's collective experience gets pointed at your problem — and yours at theirs. What you leave with is a sharper sense of what actually works, learned from people who tried it.

It's the Homebrew Computer Club, updated: a room of people with different skills, tinkering together, comparing notes on what actually works — and building taste for what's real versus what just sounds good.

two cohorts

The same format, calibrated to the room. The cohorts alternate weeks, so yours meets every other week — twice a month — and you're always with peers.

int
Intermediate
You use AI daily and have wired up a few things — a custom GPT, a script, an automation — but you're not running agents in production. Demos are pitched so you can follow them and steal from them.
adv
Advanced
You run agents, pipelines, and loops every day. Demos go to full depth — architecture, failure modes, the parts that took three rewrites.

what it isn't

  • Not a course. No syllabus, no sequence, nothing to catch up on.
  • Not a lecture. Nobody stands at the front and explains AI to you.
  • Not a spectator sport. Everyone shows work eventually. That's how the room builds its taste — including yours.

a typical session

1
Three demos
~15 min each, live — the actual tool running, not a recording
2
What worked. What broke.
Everyone names one thing that paid off this month and one that didn't. The wins tell you what's worth copying; the failures save you the month it would have cost to find out yourself.
3
Open discussion
Go deep on a specific project, get unstuck, or take on whatever just landed in AI — a new model, a new tool, a story worth arguing about
